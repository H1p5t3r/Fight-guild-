    WORK ANYWHERE

    Your moves. Your browser.

    1. Choose a fighter and move. Turn Attack enabled off to remove an unused command normal from play without deleting its data. Tune numbers, routes or hitbox dimensions.
    2. Use Art brief to describe poses and effects, and attach pose-reference photos with captions.
    3. Download JSON to keep a backup or move between PCs.
    4. At home, import it through the Godot move editor, or send the JSON back here for the next sprite pass.

    Drafts are saved in this browser when storage is available. They do not sync between devices, and clearing browser data removes the local draft.

    The hosted editor loads its assets from the website. Edits stay in your browser. The portable ZIP works offline after extraction, without installing anything. The shared attack workspace runs approach and command inputs, contact, hitstop and target reactions. Pause or scrub its timeline, then drag hitboxes in Hitboxes & impact or body boxes in Hurtboxes & defense. Use Regions that can be hit to turn each hurtbox on or off by phase without deleting its size. Edits update the same scene and keep the selected move frame. Disabled or unmapped attacks show labelled, editable geometry without simulated contacts. Artwork uses the current idle, walk, run and attack poses. Poses display directly without added transition frames; editing values does not regenerate the source sprite sheets.

    In Hitboxes & impact, add active-frame keys to change hitboxes over time. Multiple boxes count as one hit by default. Turn on Allow multiple hits and use different hit numbers for separate hits; boxes with the same number connect only once per attack.

    Distances shows horizontal root spacing, the gap between pushboxes (or their overlap), the forward edge of the visible attack shape, and the selected box’s size. The ground ruler starts at the attacker’s root, with positive values forward. These are game pixels; camera zoom does not change them. A horizontal range alone does not guarantee a hit.

    On a phone, tap the Move picker to search the kit and use the section selector beneath the preview. Options contains test setup, overlays and playback speed. Swipe the stage to scroll; in a box-editing section, tap Drag boxes to enable touch editing, then Done dragging to scroll again. Minimize makes more room for fields while keeping playback controls available.

    Find a setting… searches across all sections. Relevant fields shows controls used by the current setup; All fields also shows inactive values with explanations. Neither view deletes values. Open optional groups or use Show inactive fields to inspect more settings. Group choices are remembered; tap ? beside a field for help. Invalid edits remain visible.

    Drive system groups the fighter’s entries, stance attacks and exits. Choose an input, then edit the initial attack, applied status and repeat-input follow-up separately. Drive test buttons use the shared preview. On taller phones, Pin keeps that preview visible; otherwise it scrolls away to leave room for fields. Preview ↑ returns to it.

    Sequence test queues up to 12 attacks per fighter. Add and reorder moves, then set each Delay +f (0–120 frames) after its first legal input opportunity. Hitstop does not count toward delays. Play or scrub the same preview to inspect hits, blocks, damage and combo length. Each step reports its start frame or why its input could not run. Whiffs and illegal cancels wait for recovery; stance and follow-up prerequisites still apply. Air attacks insert a jump when grounded. Sequence queues save with Export JSON.

    Character movement edits walk/run speeds, independent 66/44 styles, jumps, air movement and Faultless Defense. Projectiles adds moving shots or stationary objects to an attack. All controls travel with Download JSON.

    Startup minimums: grabs 2 frames, P normals 3 frames, other attacks 5 frames. Positions are in game pixels; speed is pixels per frame. Existing global rules and the other fighters’ data are preserved when you edit a move.

    Pose photos, notes and sequence queues travel in the workshop JSON. Keep that file: Godot exports gameplay values only.
